# Warren Buffett's Reading List

Buffett doesn't maintain a public reading list, but he has recommended books for decades in Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters, annual meetings and interviews. This list collects the recurring recommendations.

Primary source: [Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters ↗](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html).

## Investing

- **The Intelligent Investor** — Benjamin Graham. The book Buffett calls "by far the best book on investing ever written"; chapters 8 and 20 especially.
- **Security Analysis** — Benjamin Graham & David Dodd. The foundational text; Buffett studied under Graham at Columbia.
- **Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits** — Philip Fisher. Buffett describes himself as "85% Graham, 15% Fisher".
- **Where Are the Customers' Yachts?** — Fred Schwed. A funny, skeptical classic about Wall Street that Buffett has recommended repeatedly.
- **The Little Book of Common Sense Investing** — John C. Bogle. Recommended in the shareholder letters; Buffett's standing advice to most investors is Bogle-style index investing.
- **The Essays of Warren Buffett** — Warren Buffett, arranged by Lawrence Cunningham. His own preferred distillation of the shareholder letters.

## Business and Management

- **Business Adventures** — John Brooks. Buffett lent his copy to Bill Gates in 1991; both call it their favorite business book.
- **The Outsiders** — William N. Thorndike. Eight unconventional CEOs and their capital-allocation playbooks; recommended in the 2012 shareholder letter.
- **Jack: Straight from the Gut** — Jack Welch. Recommended in the shareholder letters when it came out.
- **Shoe Dog** — Phil Knight. The Nike founder's memoir; Buffett praised it as one of the best books he'd read.
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** — Charlie Munger (ed. Peter Kaufman). The collected wisdom of his longtime partner.
